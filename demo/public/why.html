Why label-cache?

RDF names everything with IRIs. Turning those IRIs into words is the same boring problem in every app — and most ways of solving it are bad.

RDF identifies everything with an IRI: skos:broader, https://schema.org/jobTitle, wd:Q42. They're globally unique but unreadable. Any app that shows RDF to a human has to resolve IRI → label — over and over, for predicates, classes, and entities. That lookup is identical everywhere, yet it's usually solved in ways that don't scale.

Data Your app — query → Knowledge graph ← results (opaque IRIs)
Labels Your app — GET /label?iri=… → Label cache → edge cache → R2
Labels come from a globally distributed edge CDN, not your triplestore. Popular vocabulary IRIs are a cache hit almost every time, so the knowledge graph only ever serves your data.

The usual ways to get labels — and why they hurt

1. Hard-code labels into the app

Ship a big { iri: "label" } table in the frontend.

Why it's bad: stale the moment a vocabulary changes, duplicated in every app you build, no shared source of truth, needs a redeploy to fix a typo — and it only covers the IRIs you thought of in advance.

2. Ask for labels in the query (complicated SPARQL)

OPTIONAL-join rdfs:label / skos:prefLabel, with language fallbacks, for every variable you project.

Why it's bad: the query balloons — every term needs its own OPTIONAL + COALESCE, language fallback in SPARQL is miserable, it's slow, and you paste the same label boilerplate into every query you ever write.

3. A follow-up query for the labels

Run the main query, collect the IRIs it returned, then query the graph again for their labels.

Why it's bad: the right shape — this is exactly what label-cache does — but it always hits your triplestore: uncached, extra load, a round trip per batch. And the labels for common terms like rdfs:label or schema:name are identical across every graph on earth, yet you re-fetch them from your KG every single time.

4. Shadow “label” named graphs

Materialise a named graph — e.g. one per focus node — that holds all the labels for its IRIs, so you can pull them alongside the data.

Why it's bad: now you maintain a copy. It drifts from the source, bloats storage, needs a sync/materialisation pipeline to stay current — and it's still inside the triplestore, so every read still lands on the KG.

What label-cache does instead

Follow-up lookups — from an edge CDN, not your KG

Take approach 3 (a per-IRI lookup) and move it off the triplestore entirely. Labels are pre-materialised once into blob storage (R2) and fronted by a tiered edge cache. A lookup is a plain GET /label?iri=…, resolved at the nearest edge location; popular vocabulary IRIs are served from cache without ever touching a database. Your triplestore serves data; the CDN serves labels. They're decoupled.

Populating it — for the demo, and for you

Nothing is pre-loaded by magic. You populate R2 once with the labels your data needs — the same way this demo was populated. Two things go in:

  • Your own IRIs — their annotation properties (labels, descriptions, …), extracted from your data.
  • The public vocabulary terms you actually use — the handful of Schema.org / SKOS / Dublin Core predicates and classes your data references, without loading whole ontologies you don't need.

At triplestore scale, a single SPARQL CONSTRUCT can do both: find every IRI your data mentions and emit its label and description from wherever they're defined. That dump feeds the same generate → upload step the demo used. (Prefer whole public vocabularies over stripping to just what's used? Storage is cheap — load the lot.) Recipes and scripts for this live in the repo.
